## Diagnosis Of The Original Version

The current introduction opens with consensus versus majority as the main puzzle. That framing is correct for the existing formal comparison, but it understates the more valuable contribution identified by both reviews: pivotality is an independent source of informational power when the pivotal actor is powerful and privately informed.

The draft already contains the right ingredients: `pi_H=0`, weak-state agenda control, private thresholds, fixed-pie packages, and the contrast between exclusion under majority and non-excludability under unanimity. The problem is ordering. The introduction currently treats `pi_H=0` as a baseline assumption and consensus as the main object. The revised lead treats `pi_H=0` as the paper's conceptual move and treats majority/unanimity as the comparison that reveals the mechanism.

## Main Changes

1. Reframed the opening around institutional non-excludability rather than consensus alone.
   - The new first paragraph covers consensus rules, veto systems, and weighted-voting arrangements where a powerful actor cannot be bypassed.

2. Made pivotality the causal mechanism.
   - The new lead states the causal chain directly: institutional necessity -> private threshold matters -> weak proposer screens or pools -> informational rent.

3. Recast `pi_H=0` as the paper's design choice, not a mere baseline.
   - The revised introduction says that agenda power is deliberately removed so any gain for the hegemon must come from pivotality and private information.

4. Repositioned majority versus unanimity.
   - Majority becomes the bypass benchmark; unanimity becomes the formal representation of institutional non-excludability.

5. Broadened the scope without changing the formal model.
   - The revised text mentions veto systems and weighted voting as settings where the same logic can apply, while keeping the formal comparison tied to majority and unanimity.

6. Preserved the formal caveats.
   - The weak-vote-passive assessment, selected-PBE language, No-Cheap-H condition, entry nesting, and conditional payoff comparison remain in the introduction.

## What Was Preserved

1. The core formal results: majority no-screening under No-Cheap-H, unanimity R1 selected outcomes in `P/L/R`, entry nesting, and the payoff-gap classification.

2. The OPEC illustration and its non-calibration status.

3. The distinction between agenda power, outside-option power, and pivotality.

4. The conditional nature of the institutional ranking. The revised version does not claim that consensus always benefits a hegemon.

## Points For Author Verification

1. Scope: the new lead says the logic also applies to veto systems and weighted-voting arrangements where a powerful actor cannot be bypassed. This is consistent with your new framing, but the formal model still uses majority/unanimity. Confirm whether this broader language should remain in the introduction or be moved to the discussion.

2. Terminology: I used "institutional non-excludability" as the bridge concept between consensus, veto power, and weighted voting. You may prefer "pivotality" alone, "non-bypassability," or "institutional necessity."

3. Title implication: the current title still works, but the new lead suggests a possible tighter title:
   - `Informational Power Through Pivotality`
   - `Pivotality and Informational Power`
   - `Private Thresholds and Institutional Power`

4. Abstract implication: the abstract should be rewritten after the introduction is approved. It should lead with pivotality as a general mechanism and mention consensus/majority as the institutional comparison.

5. Downstream consistency: if this framing is approved, the paper should replace recurring language that treats `pi_H=0` as merely the "baseline." Better terms include "main design," "core model," or "weak-state-agenda design."
