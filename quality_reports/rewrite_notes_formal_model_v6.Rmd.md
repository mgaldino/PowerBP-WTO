# Editorial notes: Informational Power Through Pivotality: How Consensus Can Benefit a Hegemon

**Original file**: `formal_model_v6.Rmd`
**Date**: 2026-08-31
**Status**: proposal approved and applied during the controlled manuscript transition at commit base `e8e82fc`

## Diagnosis of the original version

The existing introduction contains the right puzzle and a careful statement of
the formal results, but the intellectual sequence is diluted by three problems.
It describes the United States as having no agenda power in the WTO, which
confuses the paper's identifying benchmark with the historical institution; it
compresses several distinct literatures into the introduction; and it leaves
the WTO beside an OPEC illustration that no longer fits the direction of the
paper.

## Main changes

- Preserved the WTO hook and sharpened the tension between sovereign equality
  in the formal rules and material inequality in the international system.
- Replaced the claim that the United States had no agenda power with the exact
  benchmark claim: the WTO gave it no exclusive formal proposal right, while
  Steinberg documents substantial de facto agenda influence.
- Used Steinberg's account of the Uruguay Round to microfound the model's
  disagreement payoff. The single undertaking packages the contract; U.S. and
  European Community withdrawal from GATT 1947 changes the fallback. The two
  operations remain analytically distinct.
- Stated the model as an identifying counterfactual rather than a literal
  description of WTO bargaining.
- Moved detailed engagement with Baron--Ferejohn, Kalandrakis, Piazolo--Vanberg,
  Glynia--Thum--Xefteris, and the remaining bargaining literature to a proposed
  standalone literature section.
- Narrowed the novelty claim. The paper does not claim to discover agenda power,
  exclusion, signaling through rejection, or insurance against costly voters.
  Its contribution is the asymmetric composition margin and the type-specific
  decomposition of public pivotality and informational rent.
- Preserved the exact limits of the v6 model: the conditional signs, the
  type-specific incidence, and the empty intermediate-belief correspondence.
- Replaced the proposed OPEC application with a WTO creation/GATT fallback
  application in a separate staging document.

## What was preserved

- The opening substantive object is the WTO.
- The motivating inconsistency remains formal equality among states alongside
  unequal power in the international system.
- The hegemon has no exclusive proposal right in the benchmark.
- Coalition substitutability remains the mechanism.
- Informational rent remains a private-minus-public, type-specific object.
- No payoff is imputed where the maintained pure-strategy correspondence is
  empty.
- Consensus and unanimity continue to denote the same approval rule in the
  model, subject to the paper's expected-indifference convention.
- The introduction itself contains no formula imported from \(A_T\). After the
  proposal was written, the closing commit and final manifest arrived and the
  author expressly released `MIG-AT-01` through `MIG-AT-05` and `MIG-SEM-03`.
  Those lines were consumed separately in the agenda-extension section and
  Appendices E--F from the frozen source at commit base `e8e82fc`.

## Points for author verification

1. The opening says that the United States "led the creation" of the WTO, in
   keeping with the requested hook. If a more distributed historical
   formulation is preferred, "helped create" is available without changing the
   argument.
2. The GATT sentence follows Steinberg's account: the United States and European
   Community joined the WTO and then withdrew from GATT 1947, terminating the
   old MFN fallback vis-a-vis countries that rejected the Final Act. The text
   presents this as an implemented exit threat, not as a constitutive feature of
   the single undertaking.
3. The introduction proposal kept the then-current title. The later full
   migration authorization applied the separate title decision, *Power and Its
   Shadow: When Unanimity Serves the Hegemon*.
4. The roadmap assumes that the proposed literature section will be inserted
   between the introduction and the numerical illustration.
5. The application deliberately uses "consistent with" logic. It does not claim
   that the model identifies why the United States created the WTO or that the
   historical United States was a particular private type.
6. The proposed version boundary is an annotated Git tag after application,
   compilation, and independent review. No tag is created during this proposal
   phase.
