### V2: RESOLVED --- original deduction: -3

The "Why simultaneous entry?" paragraph (line 594) provides a clear methodological justification: "The all-or-nothing entry technology isolates the screening mechanism by suppressing dynamics that would arise under sequential accession---such as free-riding, learning from early joiners, or collective action problems among weak states." It further notes that "with sequential entry, multiple mechanisms would operate in opposing directions, and the net effect on the hegemon's institutional preference would depend on their relative magnitudes." This is a legitimate scope argument: the model isolates one channel, acknowledges that others exist, and does not claim to capture them.

**Residual deduction: 0.** The fix is adequate.

### V3: RESOLVED --- original deduction: -8

This was the highest-priority revision, and the fix is substantial. Two new paragraphs in the Discussion (lines 584--586) now provide:

1. **A discriminating empirical prediction** ("the selection channel"): the model predicts a negative association between consensus rules and transparent distributive stakes. Financial institutions (where the pie is measurable) should use weighted voting; regulatory coordination bodies (where consequences are harder to forecast) should adopt consensus. This is specific, testable, and distinguishes the informational mechanism from alternatives.

2. **Explicit contrast with three alternative accounts**: "Legitimacy accounts predict consensus where binding obligations require broad-based assent, without conditioning on whether the distributive consequences of cooperation are transparent or opaque. Self-binding accounts predict consensus where commitment problems are most severe, regardless of informational structure. Informal power accounts predict that powerful states exercise influence irrespective of the formal voting rule, and therefore cannot explain why consensus is systematically absent in financial organizations where powerful states have the strongest informal leverage."

3. **Consistency with data**: "Existing data on the voting rules of international organizations [@gould2022consensus] are consistent with this pattern---financial institutions overwhelmingly use weighted majority voting, while organizations concerned with regulatory coordination are the most frequent adopters of consensus."

4. **Additional predictions**: capacity development (screening advantage diminishes as weak states invest in analysis) and PTAs (proliferation raises alpha toward alpha*).

The original V3 demanded a systematic differentiation. This has been delivered. The only minor residual is that the contrast with Maggi & Morelli (self-enforcement) is bundled under "self-binding accounts" rather than named specifically, and the "veto-as-insurance" and "renegotiation-proofness" alternatives from my Round 1 list are not individually addressed. However, these are subsumed by the broader argument that the model generates predictions conditional on informational structure, while the alternatives do not. This is sufficient for JoP.

**Residual deduction: -1.** (Minor: Maggi & Morelli not cited by name in the differentiation paragraph, though cited elsewhere in the paper. A referee working specifically on self-enforcing agreements might want a more targeted contrast.)

## New Issues Introduced by Revisions

### N1. Selection channel prediction may be slightly overstrong (Minor: -1)

The Discussion (line 584) predicts that "organizations that combine unanimity with transparent distributive stakes are unstable." The word "unstable" implies a dynamic that is not modeled (the static model has no exit or rule-change mechanism). A more cautious phrasing ("less likely to adopt or sustain consensus") would be more precise. The prediction itself is compelling and well-differentiated from alternatives; the concern is only about the strength of the verb.

**Deduction: -1.**
