## 3. Thesis, question, and contribution

**Thesis.** Conditional on the declared equilibrium architecture and its existence domains, unanimity can favor a privately informed hegemon even when weak states control proposals because unanimity removes coalition substitutes for the hegemon's approval; public-type comparisons isolate the value of pivotality, private-minus-public comparisons isolate informational rent, and a separate mandatory agenda stage shows how proposal power interacts with information.

**Research question.** What happens to a powerful actor's bargaining payoff and informational rent when majority can bypass the only privately informed participant but unanimity makes that participant's approval indispensable, and how does an earlier mandatory proposal opportunity alter that comparison?

**Contribution.** The paper reorganizes private information around one hegemon and symmetric uninformed weak states. It treats the approval rule as an on/off switch for the informed participant, distinguishes public pivotality from type-specific informational rent, preserves linked type vectors and empty equilibrium cells, and adds a correspondence-valued agenda extension with the identities \(\Delta V^A=\Delta v^A+\Delta IR^A\) and \(T_g=D_g+I_g\). The agenda extension is separate from, not a replacement for, the identifying no-agenda benchmark. The WTO material is a theoretical consistency crosswalk rather than empirical identification.

The claims are exact formal equilibrium characterizations, set-valued comparative claims, and structural within-model contrasts. They are not empirically identified causal estimates.

## 4. Core claims

1. **C01 — Essential-input thesis.** Majority can replace the informed hegemon with uninformed weak-state votes; unanimity cannot. This changes both public bargaining power and how private information enters offers. The institutional ranking is conditional, not universal. (PDF pp. 1–3, 30–34.)
2. **C02 — Public benchmark.** With public disagreement payoff \(o\), Round-1 majority includes \(H\) iff \(o\le 1/m\), with inclusion selected at equality, whereas unanimity pays \(\beta o\). Thus \(v_M^B(o)=\beta o\) for \(o\le1/m\) and \(o\) otherwise, while \(v_U^B(o)=\beta o\). (Proposition 5.1, PDF pp. 12–13; proof p. 36.)
3. **C03 — Terminal private games.** Terminal majority excludes \(H\). Terminal unanimity screens with \(x_H=\ell\) for \(p\le p^*=(h-\ell)/(1-\ell)\), with the low offer selected at equality, and pools with \(x_H=h\) above the cutoff. (Proposition 5.2, PDF pp. 13–14; proof p. 36.)
4. **C04 — Round-1 private majority.** The exact correspondence is organized by exclusion, screening, and pooling, with cells determined by \(\ell,h\) relative to \(1/m\) and by \(p_{S=E}\) or \(p_{S=P}\). Deliberate delay is dominated in this baseline arm. Residual multiplicity at \(h=1/m\) is linked by one common proposal weight. (Proposition 5.3, PDF pp. 14–16; proof pp. 37–38.)
5. **C05 — Round-1 private unanimity.** Under the maintained PBE with pure ballot strategies and declared belief/voting disciplines, immediate low-type agreement exists at \(p=0\), the correspondence is empty for \(0<p\le p^*\), and immediate pooling exists for \(p>p^*\). The empty cell is not a claim about mixed ballot strategies. (Proposition 5.4, PDF pp. 15–16; proof pp. 38–39.)
6. **C06 — Baseline payoff and rent contrasts.** \(V_U^B-V_M^B\), \(IR_g^B=V_g^B-v_g^B\), and \(\Delta IR^B=IR_U^B-IR_M^B\) are distinct linked, type-contingent correspondences. They can be positive, zero, negative, set-valued, or empty depending on the cell; no unconditional unanimity advantage follows. (Propositions 5.5–5.7, PDF pp. 17–23; proofs pp. 39–40.)
7. **C07 — Public agenda effects.** With an earlier mandatory hegemonic proposal, \(v_U^A(o)=1-\beta+\beta^2o\). Public majority buys a minimal coalition or deliberately delays according to \(o_M^*=v_M^{\mathrm{safe}}/\beta\). The public gap is negative for \(o\le1/m\) and, for \(o>1/m\), has the sign of \(\beta o-e/m\). (PDF pp. 23–25; Appendix E.6–E.8, pp. 52–54.)
8. **C08 — Private agenda correspondences.** The agenda extension preserves full Borel proposal laws, pure ballots, posterior fibers, literal frozen continuations, outcomes, and linked low/high payoffs in complete binders. Majority has pooling, separation, agreement, and delay classes plus Borel mixtures; unanimity has exact low- and high-posterior families and endpoint correspondences. Payoff images and economic summaries do not replace binders. (Appendix E.1–E.4, PDF pp. 43–51.)
9. **C09 — Same-fiber institutional comparison.** Private agenda comparisons pair complete majority and unanimity records from the same primitives and off-path fiber, preserve type vectors before ex ante aggregation, and propagate empty sources. If \(\beta h<e/m\), every comparable member favors majority for both types and ex ante; this condition is sufficient, not necessary. (PDF pp. 25–26; Appendix E.4–E.5, pp. 50–52.)
10. **C10 — Public power and information decomposition.** Member by member, \(\Delta V^A=\Delta v^A+\Delta IR^A\). In every existing unanimity agenda fiber, \(IR_U^A(\ell)\ge0\) and \(IR_U^A(h)\le0\), with at least one strict inequality. A private low-type unanimity advantage against a negative public gap requires relative informational rent to offset that gap. (PDF pp. 26–27; Appendix E.9–E.10, pp. 54–55.)
11. **C11 — Structural agenda decomposition.** At date \(A\), \(D_g=v_g^A-\beta v_g^B\), \(I_g=IR_g^A-\beta IR_g^B\), and \(T_g=V_g^A-\beta V_g^B\), so \(T_g=D_g+I_g\). Wherever both arms exist, every \(T_U\) member weakly benefits both types; no general sign is imposed on \(T_M\). \(Q_g=v_g^A-\beta V_g^B\) changes agenda and information simultaneously and is not a one-factor causal effect. (PDF pp. 27–29; Appendix E.11–E.14 and F.2–F.4, pp. 55–62.)
12. **C12 — WTO scope.** WTO practices are mapped to recognition, disagreement, contract-space, information, repeated-interaction, and implementation primitives. The episode is not a literal implementation, calibration, or identification of the model; consensus is an application-level analogue of indispensable approval, not a claim that the WTO reproduces the formal protocol. (PDF pp. 31–33, 62–64.)

## 6. Profile-specific contract

### Players

- One hegemon \(H\).
- \(m\ge3\) symmetric weak states \(W=\{1,\ldots,m\}\), for \(m+1\) players total.
- Nature selects \(H\)'s terminal disagreement payoff \(o\in\{\ell,h\}\), with \(0<\ell<h<1\).

### Timing

1. Nature selects \(o\); only \(H\) observes it.
2. In each of two baseline rounds, a weak state is recognized uniformly and independently with replacement; \(H\) never proposes.
3. The proposer chooses a feasible package and counts as voting yes; all responders vote simultaneously, and the complete ballot becomes public only after voting closes.
4. Passage implements the package. Round-1 failure yields no current payoff and moves to Round 2. Round-2 failure yields terminal disagreement.
5. The agenda extension adds date \(A\), before Round 1, at which informed \(H\) must propose. Passage is immediate; rejection enters the full frozen Round-1 continuation discounted exactly once. \(H\) cannot skip the stage while retaining a date-A continuation.

### Information and beliefs

- Prior \(p=\Pr(o=h)\in[0,1]\). Weak states have no private signal about \(o\).
- Weak-state proposals and votes do not move the public belief about \(o\).
- Actions by \(H\) update by Bayes where possible; zero-denominator actions at interior priors allow posteriors in \([0,1]\) subject to the manuscript's declared `structural consistency` restriction.
- Endpoint support never expands: \(p=0\) and \(p=1\) are literal complete-information games, not one-sided limits.
- In the agenda extension, disciplined proposals use local Bayes likelihood ratios; undisciplined proposals use \(\mu^{\mathrm{off}}=b_\rho(p)\), with endpoint fibers \((*,p)\).

### Actions and strategies

- Baseline weak proposer chooses \(x=(x_H,(x_j)_{j\in W})\); responders choose yes/no.
- Under majority, passage needs \(k=\lfloor(m+1)/2\rfloor\) additional yes votes beyond the proposer; unanimity requires all players.
- A no vote is only a ballot action. It is not opt-out and does not itself trigger disagreement.
- In equilibrium exclusion, \(x_H=0\), even though the complete-game branch after majority passage and a no vote pays \(x_H+o\).
- Ballot strategies are pure under the maintained solution concept. Agenda-stage proposal laws may be arbitrary Borel mixtures, including atomless supports. The abstract/main-text shorthand `pure-strategy correspondence` or `pure-PBE M/S/B architecture` must not be expanded into purity of all proposal strategies.
- Complete binders preserve proposal laws, posteriors, ballots, literal continuations, payoffs, and realized laws. Marginal envelopes and economic summaries cannot be spliced into new members.

### Equilibrium concept

Perfect Bayesian equilibrium with pure ballot strategies, plus: no signaling by players who do not know \(o\); Bayes and the declared off-path structural-consistency discipline after actions by \(H\); as-if-pivotal voting by weak responders; yes at exact expected-value indifference; type-contingent optimization by \(H\); support-preserving endpoints; and a proposal tie-break that, among proposer-payoff ties, minimizes \(H\)'s expected payoff. The agenda extension retains pure ballots but permits Borel mixed proposal laws.

### Assumptions and scope conditions

- Fixed unit pie; nonnegative package shares; no side payments outside the package; no intrinsic agreement benefit for \(H\).
- \(m\ge3\), \(0<\ell<h<1\), \(0<\beta<1\), two types and two baseline rounds.
- Simultaneous public ballots; fixed voting rule in each comparison.
- Benchmark proposals are controlled by weak states; agenda power is reintroduced only through the separate mandatory date-A stage.
- The common contrast orientation is unanimity minus majority.
- Type vectors remain linked; ex ante averaging occurs only after the linked vector is formed.
- Empty sources propagate \(\varnothing\), never zero, `NA`, infinity, or interpolation.
- Selection-free claims require the entire exact correspondence to lie on one side of the relevant threshold.
- The manuscript excludes endogenous rule choice, endogenous agenda protocol, more than two types or rounds, sequential roll-call voting, \(m<3\), \(\beta=1\), intrinsic agreement benefits, mixed-ballot characterization, repeated-game reputation, ratification, implementation, and plurilateralism.

### Propositions and exact result families

- Propositions 5.1–5.7: public baseline, private terminal games, private majority, private unanimity, private institutional payoff contrast, rule-specific informational rents, and institutional informational-rent contrast.
- Appendix C: endpoint equivalence, linked set geometry, marginal envelopes, and a non-calibrated worked illustration.
- Appendix E.2–E.3: complete private agenda correspondences for majority and unanimity.
- Appendix E.4–E.5: same-fiber institutional comparison and a sufficient majority-advantage region.
- Appendix E.6–E.14: public agenda classes, public gap, rent translations, decomposition identities, direct and total effects, and diagonal comparison.
- Appendix F: exact/economic factorization, interaction, source-existence rules, and structural causal scope.

### Mechanisms

1. **Substitution under majority:** a weak proposer can buy an uninformed weak-state vote instead of the informed hegemon.
2. **Essential input under unanimity:** every approval is required, making \(H\)'s privately known participation threshold unavoidable.
3. **Pooling rent:** when unanimity prices the package for the high threshold, the low type can receive the same concession.
4. **Dynamic continuation:** delay discounts Round-2 values by \(\beta\), creating timing wedges between current exclusion payoffs and discounted inclusion prices.
5. **Agenda power:** mandatory early proposal permits \(H\) to buy a coalition or induce delay, with different public branches across rules.
6. **Information–agenda interaction:** the agenda stage changes informational rent, producing \(I_g\) and the identity \(T_g=D_g+I_g\).
7. **Support-preserving endpoints and empty cells:** degenerate priors are complete-information games; failure of maintained pure ballot responses produces empty correspondences rather than fictional payoffs.

### Comparative statics and sign classifications

- Terminal unanimity changes from screening to pooling as \(p\) crosses \(p^*\); the low offer is selected at equality.
- Round-1 majority changes among screening, pooling, and exclusion at the stated \(p_{S=P}\) and \(p_{S=E}\) cutoffs, conditional on \(\ell,h\) relative to \(1/m\).
- Round-1 unanimity is empty on \(0<p\le p^*\) in the maintained pure-ballot domain.
- In baseline exclusion comparisons, the low-type sign can turn on \(\beta h-\ell\); high-type rent changes only when private and public majority treatment differ.
- In the public agenda game, \(\Delta v^A(o)<0\) for \(o\le1/m\); for \(o>1/m\), its sign is that of \(\beta o-e/m\).
- \(\beta h<e/m\) is sufficient, not necessary, for a setwise private majority advantage in every common nonempty agenda fiber.
- Agenda weakly reduces unanimity's informational rent in every existing high-prior fiber; it weakly benefits both types under unanimity wherever treatment and control both exist.
- No general sign is asserted for \(T_M\), \(\Delta IR^A\), \(\Delta I\), or \(\Delta T\).
- The paper supplies cutoff and sign classifications, not global derivative comparative statics in \(m,\beta,\ell,h,p\).
