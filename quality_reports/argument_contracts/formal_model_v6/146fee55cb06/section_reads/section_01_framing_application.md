## S01 — Abstract and Introduction (PDF pp. 1-3)

### Thesis of the unit

Formally equal approval rules can favor a powerful actor through a mechanism distinct from exclusive proposal power. In the benchmark, majority can replace the privately informed hegemon with uninformed weak-state votes, whereas unanimity makes the hegemon's approval indispensable. Comparing private games to public-type counterparts separates pivotality from informational rent. A separate mandatory agenda-stage extension then studies how proposal power interacts with information.

### Explicit claims

| Claim | Locator | Evidence in the unit | Scope / hedge |
|---|---|---|---|
| The paper isolates one channel by which formal vote equality can coexist with bargaining inequality. | p. 1, Abstract | Baseline contrast between replaceability under majority and indispensable approval under unanimity. | One isolated channel, not a complete theory of institutional power. |
| The public-type comparison separates the value of a necessary vote from type-specific informational rent. | p. 1, Abstract | Each private game is compared with its same-type public counterpart. | Type-specific; the ranking is conditional. |
| Some maintained pure-strategy correspondence cells are empty. | p. 1, Abstract | Empty belief regions are retained rather than assigned a payoff. | Restricted to the maintained equilibrium architecture. |

## Cross-unit synthesis for the macro reader

### Source-bound overarching thesis

The paper's identifying claim is conditional: unanimity can favor a privately informed hegemon without giving it exclusive formal proposal power because unanimity removes coalition substitutes for its approval. Public-type comparisons isolate pivotality; private/public differences isolate informational rent; the separate mandatory agenda extension decomposes the structural agenda effect as `T = D + I`. Outside options, proposal rights, and indispensable approval are distinct channels, and all rankings remain bounded by linked correspondences, domains, and equilibrium existence.

### Consolidated non-affirmations

- No universal ranking of unanimity versus majority.
- No claim that private information always benefits the hegemon.
- No payoff or interpolation in empty cells.
- No characterization of mixed-ballot equilibria in the baseline empty region.
- No claim that agenda power was historically absent from the WTO.
- No identification of WTO design motives or historical actor types.
- No empirical calibration or test in the numerical illustrations.
- No attribution of the formal model to Steinberg.
- No endogenous rule choice, endogenous agenda protocol, repeated reputation, implementation, ratification, or plurilateral extension.

### Ambiguities requiring macro disposition

1. Incomplete `I show that..` sentence (p. 3).
2. Broad versus formal/de facto uses of `agenda power` (pp. 2-5, 32, 62).
3. Undefined `M/S/B` acronym in the assigned main-text scope discussion (p. 33).
4. Applied `consensus` versus formal `unanimity` should be treated as an analogue, not an unqualified institutional identity.
5. The WTO crosswalk motivates disagreement values for major actors and partners, while the private modeled object is specifically the hegemon's disagreement payoff.
