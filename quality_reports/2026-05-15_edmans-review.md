# Editorial Letter - Edmans Framework Review

## Decision: R&R major

## Consolidated Scores

| Dimension | Score | Rating |
|---|---:|---|
| Contribution | 7/10 | Promising, but needs sharper differentiation |
| Execution | 7/10 | Credible conditional theory |
| Exposition | 8/10 | Clear, but dense |
| **Global** | **7.3/10** | **Strong revise** |

## Editorial Synthesis

The paper has a real contribution: it shows how consensus can empower a hegemon through informational pivotality even when agenda power is deliberately removed. This is a meaningful update to the standard intuition that consensus protects weak states or constrains power. The best version of the paper is a clean theoretical mechanism, not an empirical account of OPEC.

The main challenge is positioning. Incomplete-information bargaining, screening rents, veto players, and consensus are familiar ingredients. The paper therefore needs to make unmistakable what the reader learns that was not already implicit in that literature: the separation of agenda power from pivotality, and the claim that formal equality can itself create asymmetric informational rents.

The execution is credible as conditional formal theory. The manuscript mostly avoids overclaiming: it treats OPEC as an illustration, keeps rule choice exogenous, and states the R1 result as a selected PBE outcome under the weak-vote-passive assessment. The exposition is strong, but the current draft lets proof-audit discipline spill into the article body through dense abstracts, script-heavy captions, and many diagnostic tables.

## Edmans Hierarchy Applied

The contribution is strong enough to merit revision. It is not yet strong enough to coast on execution alone. The revision should sharpen novelty and importance first, then tighten execution and exposition around that contribution. More robustness or more extensions will not help unless they clarify the central update: consensus can benefit a hegemon precisely when it denies agenda power but makes the hegemon's private participation threshold pivotal.

## Priorities For Revision

1. Add a short "what readers learn" paragraph in the introduction. It should explain why this is not just a standard screening result in new institutional clothing.

2. Treat `pi_H=0` as the conceptual engine of the paper. State explicitly that it stacks agenda power against the hegemon and therefore isolates pivotality.

3. Add an assumption-to-result table distinguishing primitive assumptions, domain restrictions, equilibrium-assessment restrictions, and numerical-illustration choices.

4. Keep the OPEC section illustrative unless adding separate empirical evidence. Do not imply historical validation from normalized numerical examples.

5. Shorten and de-technicalize the abstract. Keep the mechanism, institutional contrast, and one numerical takeaway; move weak-vote-passive and selected-PBE detail out of the abstract.

6. Remove script paths from main-text captions and move provenance to the reproducibility ledger.

7. Replace "working calibration" in generated figure titles with "working numerical illustration" and replace informal phrases such as "KISS restriction" with journal-style language.

## Strategic Recommendation To The Author

Revise for a formal-theory-friendly political science or IO/IPE outlet. The paper should not be expanded into a broader empirical paper unless the author wants a different project. Its best path is to become a leaner theory article with sharper contribution framing, a cleaner assumption map, and less visible proof-repair scaffolding.

## Full Review - Contribution

# Contribution Review (Edmans Framework)

## Score: 7/10

## Summary of the Alleged Contribution

The paper argues that consensus can benefit a hegemon not by giving it agenda power, but by making its privately known participation threshold pivotal. The formal contribution is to separate outside-option power, veto/pivotality power, and proposal power in a fixed-pie bargaining model where weak states control proposals and the hegemon has zero recognition probability.

## Evaluation by Dimension

### Novelty [Adequate]

The core mechanism is not entirely new: incomplete-information bargaining, veto players, screening rents, and consensus bargaining are well-established. A skeptical formal-theory reader could say that if an uninformed proposer must buy approval from a privately informed veto player, pooling rents are predictable.

The paper's genuine novelty is the institutional-design reframing: it removes hegemonic agenda power by setting `pi_H = 0` and still shows that consensus can benefit the hegemon through pivotality alone. That separation of agenda power from informational veto power is the paper's strongest contribution. The contribution is therefore real, but it needs to work harder to show that the result is not just a clean application of known screening logic to IO consensus rules.

### Importance [Adequate]

The question is important. Consensus rules in international organizations are substantively central, and the paper challenges a common intuition that unanimity mainly protects weak states. The implication that formal equality can generate asymmetric informational power is potentially survey-worthy for IO, IPE, and formal institutional design.

The limitation is that the paper currently offers a mechanism and classification, not yet a broad substantive reorientation. The OPEC illustration helps, but because it is explicitly not calibrated, the practical decision relevance remains suggestive rather than decisive. A policymaker would not yet change institutional design based on the model, but an IO scholar could update how they think about consensus.

### Scope Fit [Adequate]

The paper fits International Organization, AJPS-style formal theory, and IPE audiences better than a broad APSR/JOP audience. The bibliography is appropriately anchored in rational design, informal power in IOs, legislative bargaining, veto bargaining, and OPEC. The paper is clearly in the right intellectual neighborhood.

The main scope risk is that the result may be too specialized unless the introduction more aggressively explains why "informational power through pivotality" travels beyond OPEC-style bargaining.

### Generalizability [Adequate]

The mechanism is general in principle: any setting with a privately informed pivotal actor, costly institutional concessions, and weak proposers can generate similar screening rents. The paper also states scope conditions clearly: No-Cheap-H, fixed rule environment, binary types, weak-vote-passive assessment, and `pi_H = 0`.

But external validity is still bounded. The model uses symmetric weak states, fixed-pie packages, two rounds, binary types, and a maintained belief assessment. The OPEC case is useful but not enough to demonstrate that the mechanism covers WTO, IMF, EU, UNSC, climate clubs, or other consensus institutions. Generalizability is plausible, not yet fully sold.

### Trade-Offs [Partial]

The paper does consider both sides of the institutional trade-off. It does not claim that unanimity globally dominates majority; it shows entry nesting, weak-state costs, and a conditional hegemon payoff comparison through `Delta_H(mu)`. This is a strength.

The trade-off analysis is still partial because welfare, enforcement, legitimacy, participation breadth, and majority-rule feasibility are mostly held outside the model. The paper compares distributive payoffs, not the full institutional value of consensus.

### Hypotheses [Clear and Directional]

The theoretical predictions are clear and directional. The model predicts when majority eliminates screening, when unanimity induces pooling or low-only offers, when weak-state entry under unanimity is nested in majority entry, and when the hegemon prefers unanimity versus majority.

This is not a kitchen-sink paper. The mechanism is disciplined and the propositions map cleanly onto the theory. The main issue is not hypothesis clarity, but whether the assumptions supporting the selected PBE assessment are substantively compelling enough for the contribution to carry.

## Overall Verdict on Contribution

This is a promising formal-theory contribution with a clear conceptual hook: consensus can empower a hegemon through informational pivotality even without agenda power. That is a meaningful update to the usual "consensus protects the weak" intuition. However, for a top journal, the paper still needs to sharpen why this is more than an elegant application of known screening logic. The current contribution is strongest as a precise institutional-design mechanism for IO bargaining; it is weaker as a general theory of consensus unless the manuscript broadens the substantive stakes and shows more clearly where existing theories would predict differently.

## Constructive Suggestions

1. State the counterintuitive contribution more forcefully against named alternatives: self-binding, informal agenda control, and weak-state veto protection.

2. Add a short "what readers learn that they did not already know" paragraph in the introduction.

3. Broaden the scope discussion with 2-3 non-OPEC institutional examples where the primitives plausibly apply.

4. Clarify why `pi_H = 0` is not merely a simplifying assumption but the contribution-generating design move.

5. Emphasize that the key contribution is not "consensus helps hegemons," but "consensus can help hegemons precisely when it denies them agenda control yet makes their private threshold pivotal."

## Full Review - Execution

# Execution Report (Edmans Framework)

## Score: 7/10

## Type of Paper: Theoretical

## Summary of Theoretical Strategy

The paper develops a formal bargaining model comparing majority rule and unanimity when a powerful actor is privately informed about its participation threshold. The core design choice is disciplined: the hegemon has no proposal power in the baseline, so any advantage under unanimity must come from pivotality and private information rather than agenda control. The main claim is explicitly conditional: unanimity can benefit the hegemon in classified regions, not universally.

## "Data vs. Evidence" Principle

There is no empirical research design here. The relevant evidence is formal: assumptions, equilibrium assessment, propositions, proofs, and numerical diagnostics. Within the maintained model, the derivations provide evidence for the conditional theoretical claims. The OPEC discussion is plausibility evidence, not conclusive evidence that the mechanism operated historically; the manuscript mostly handles this correctly by calling the numbers a working illustration rather than a calibration.

## Evaluation by Dimension

### Distance Between Assumptions and Conclusions: Sufficient, With Reservations

The paper does not simply assume the headline result. In fact, setting `pi_H = 0` stacks agenda power against the hegemon, and the majority benchmark lets weak states exclude `H` under No-Cheap-H. That creates real distance between premise and conclusion.

The main reservation is that several maintained restrictions do important work: No-Cheap-H, the R1 Dynamic Threshold Order, High-Posterior Pooling, weak-vote-passive beliefs, and the H-payoff-minimizing tie-break. The manuscript is commendably explicit that the R1 result is a selected PBE outcome under a maintained assessment, not a characterization of all PBEs. But execution would be stronger if the paper more sharply separated "mechanism-generating assumptions" from "technical selection/accounting assumptions."

### Parsimony and Clarity: Moderately Parsimonious

The core mechanism is clear: unanimity makes `H` pivotal; private thresholds force weak proposers to screen or pool; pooling gives the low-threshold type an informational rent. The fixed-pie relative-package setup is cleaner than a transfer feasibility model and maps reasonably to quota/flexibility/enforcement terms.

The cost is complexity. The manuscript layers binary types, two rounds, passive beliefs, rejected-history classification, entry nesting, payoff classification, and numerical illustration. Most pieces are motivated, but the execution risks making the reader wonder which assumptions are essential. The working illustration also lies on the boundary `a0(1) = a1`, which blocks strict low-only separation; the paper acknowledges this, but a boundary main illustration is weaker than an interior example.

### Causal Path: Mostly Correct

The causal path is well isolated: rule -> pivotality -> screening constraint -> informational rent -> institutional ranking. The paper correctly holds agenda power out of the baseline and treats endogenous rule choice as outside scope. Entry is also handled as a weak-state participation constraint rather than smuggled into the bargaining proof.

The main vulnerability is belief updating. Weak-vote-passive assessment is substantively plausible because weak voters do not observe `H`'s type, but it is still a maintained protocol interpretation. Since R1 depends on that assessment, the paper's conclusion is precise only under that belief discipline. The manuscript recognizes this limitation, which saves the execution, but the reader should see it as central rather than secondary.

## General Verdict on Execution

The paper is well executed as a conditional formal theory paper. A reader can draw precise conclusions about the stated model: under No-Cheap-H and weak-vote-passive beliefs, majority removes screening while unanimity creates a pivotality-based screening problem, and the hegemon's ranking follows from entry sets and the payoff gap. The execution is not yet fully robust as an empirical claim about OPEC or consensus institutions generally. Its strongest version is a clean theoretical mechanism with an illustrative application, not an empirically validated account.

## Constructive Suggestions

1. Add a compact "assumption-to-result" table distinguishing primitive assumptions, domain restrictions, equilibrium-assessment restrictions, and numerical-illustration choices.

2. Use an interior numerical illustration as the main example if possible. Keep the current boundary case as a diagnostic or OPEC-style illustration, but a main example with nonempty pooling and low-only regions would better demonstrate the full R1 architecture.

3. State early that the evidentiary object is formal proof, not historical validation. The OPEC section should remain illustrative unless supported by separate empirical evidence.

4. Strengthen the discussion of weak-vote-passive beliefs by explaining what would fail or become indeterminate under alternative off-path beliefs.

5. Keep the headline claim conditional: "consensus can benefit a hegemon through pivotality-based screening," not "consensus benefits hegemons." The manuscript mostly does this well; preserving that discipline is essential.

## Full Review - Exposition

# Exposition Report (Edmans Framework)

## Score: 8/10

## Evaluation By Dimension

### Clarity [Good]

#### Writing Quality

The paper is clearly written, disciplined, and unusually careful about scope. The main claim is stated early and cleanly: consensus helps not through agenda power but because it makes the hegemon's approval an informational constraint. The introduction is strong, especially lines 54-64, where the mechanism, model choice, results, and OPEC relevance are all presented without overclaiming.

The main weakness is not grammar but density. The abstract is doing too much: `pi_H=0`, fixed-pie packages, No-Cheap-H, weak-vote-passive assessment, selected PBE, entry nesting, payoff gaps, and OPEC numbers all appear in one paragraph. A sharper abstract would reduce formal vocabulary and make the reader remember one clean mechanism first.

Suggested rewrite of the abstract core:

> I show that consensus can benefit a hegemon even when it has no proposal power. Under unanimity, weak states must offer terms without knowing the hegemon's participation threshold; under majority, they can exclude it and avoid this screening problem.

#### Substantive Significance

The paper does include memorable numbers: unanimity forms up to `0.062`, majority up to `0.083`, and the hegemon prefers unanimity below belief `0.7`. This is good. But the numbers are normalized, so their substantive meaning is not immediately intuitive.

Add one interpretive sentence:

> In the illustration, unanimity supports only about three quarters of the entry-cost range supported by majority, but within that narrower range it can still raise the hegemon's payoff when the high-threshold state is not too likely.

#### Precision Of Language

The paper is generally precise. The main claims avoid vague "institutional design matters" language and specify the direction of effects. The best example is the classification language around lines 486-552: majority preserves weak surplus; unanimity creates rents or delay; the hegemon's ranking follows `Delta_H(mu)`.

Two precision problems remain:

1. The term "working numerical illustration" is correct in the text, but the generated Figure 3 title still says "working calibration." That conflicts with the manuscript's deliberate effort not to sell the example as empirical calibration.

2. "KISS restriction" in the Scope section is too informal for APSR/AJPS/JOP/IO style. Replace it with "disciplining simplification" or "baseline restriction."

### Length [Adequate]

#### Introduction

The introduction is concise, likely under two PDF pages, and it has the right structure: question, mechanism, model design, results, classification, substantive implication. It does not waste space motivating why institutions or power matter. This is a major strength.

The one cost is that the fourth paragraph lists four results in a technically compressed way. Consider splitting the Round 1 equilibrium-assessment result from the entry-nesting result so the reader does not meet No-Cheap-H, weak-vote-passive assessment, selected PBE, and nesting all in one paragraph.

#### Footnotes

Excellent. I found no footnote burden. The paper keeps qualifications in the text or appendix rather than hiding them in notes.

#### Unnecessary Extensions

The main text has one overextended section: the numerical illustration. Lines 585-717 contain many consecutive tables and robustness checks. The content is useful, but the exposition becomes audit-like rather than article-like.

Recommendation: keep one main classification table and one payoff-gap figure in the body; move margin tables and perturbation windows to Appendix B/C. The reproducibility ledger already gives a natural home for script provenance.

### Citations [Precise And Relevant, With Minor Problems]

#### Bibliography Length

The citation footprint is restrained. The introduction cites three literatures without becoming a literature dump. This is much better than the median manuscript.

#### Specific Citation Problems

The literature paragraph at line 72 is slightly crowded. Baron-Ferejohn, proposal-rights models, information/veto bargaining, and delay bargaining are all packed into one paragraph. The connection is plausible, but the paragraph risks looking like strategic breadth.

The OPEC citations are mostly justified, but the claim that Saudi Arabia is a central swing producer and that 1985-86 matters is partly institutional background. The citations are not wrong, but the manuscript should make clear which cited works establish facts and which provide analytical interpretation.

Also, figure and table captions repeatedly include "Generated by `scripts/...`." This is good for reproducibility but bad for main-text exposition. Move those script references to the reproducibility ledger and let captions tell the reader what to learn from the figure.

## Overall Verdict On Exposition

The exposition is a strength, not a liability. The paper is clear about its mechanism, unusually careful about scope, and avoids the most dangerous overclaim: "consensus always helps the hegemon." The main exposition risk is that the manuscript sometimes lets proof-audit discipline spill into the article body. A top-journal reader should see the mechanism and classification first; scripts, boundary diagnostics, and robustness windows should support that claim from the appendix.

## Top 5 Improvement Suggestions

1. Shorten and de-technicalize the abstract. Keep the mechanism, institutional contrast, and one numerical takeaway; move "selected PBE" and "weak-vote-passive assessment" detail out of the abstract.

2. Replace "working calibration" inside generated figures with "working numerical illustration" everywhere.

3. Move most margin and robustness tables from the numerical illustration section to the appendix; keep only the classification table and payoff-gap figure in the body.

4. Remove script paths from main-text captions and keep provenance in Appendix C's reproducibility ledger.

5. Replace informal phrases like "KISS restriction" with journal-style language such as "baseline simplification" or "disciplining restriction."
