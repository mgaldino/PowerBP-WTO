# Books Combining Formal Models + Quantitative Empirics (2015-2025)

Research note: exemplars for how to present a formal model in a book-length treatment.

---

## 1. Erik Voeten — *Ideology and International Institutions*

- **Citation**: Voeten, Erik. 2021. *Ideology and International Institutions*. Princeton University Press.
- **Press**: Princeton UP
- **Topic**: How ideological conflict shapes the design, membership, and politics of international institutions.

### Structure and model presentation
- **Chapter 4 ("A Spatial Modeling Framework")** contains the formal model in the main text. The model is a spatial framework where states have ideal points in a low-dimensional ideological space with interdependent payoffs.
- **Accessibility strategy**: Voeten explicitly describes the spatial model as "as simple as the two-by-two games typically used as mental models in functionalist approaches." The model is presented *with little math* — the emphasis is on graphical intuition and verbal logic rather than algebraic derivations.
- **Proofs**: No heavy proofs; the theoretical contributions are comparative statics and propositions derived from the spatial framework, kept in the main text.
- **Empirical chapters**: Chapters 5-9 are empirical, each applying the framework to a different substantive domain (expertise & distributive politics, membership, militarized disputes, investment regime, populism/backlash against courts). Uses ideal point estimates from UNGA voting data.
- **Bridge model→empirics**: The spatial ideal points estimated in Chapters 2-3 (measurement chapters) serve as the key independent variable in the empirical chapters. The model parameters (ideal points, status quo location) map directly to measurable quantities.

### Lessons for exposition
- Model is lightweight and in the main text (1 chapter).
- No appendix proofs needed because the model is deliberately simple.
- Measurement chapters (Chs 2-3) precede the model chapter, so the reader understands the data *before* seeing the theory.
- Each empirical chapter is self-contained and applies the same framework to a new domain.

## 2. William Spaniel — *Bargaining over the Bomb: The Successes and Failures of Nuclear Negotiations*

- **Citation**: Spaniel, William. 2019. *Bargaining over the Bomb: The Successes and Failures of Nuclear Negotiations*. Cambridge University Press.
- **Press**: Cambridge UP
- **Topic**: Why nuclear negotiations succeed or fail, using bargaining theory.

### Structure and model presentation
- **Chapters alternate between theory and empirics**. The structure pairs formal model chapters with empirical/case study chapters:
  - Ch 1: Introduction
  - Ch 2: How Are Nuclear Weapons Special? (microfoundations — bargaining model of war applied to nuclear context)
  - Ch 3: The Theory of Butter-for-Bombs Agreements (core formal model with propositions and lemmas)
  - Ch 4: Does Nuclear Proficiency Induce Compliance? (quantitative empirical test)
  - Ch 5: The Diplomacy of Butter-for-Bombs Agreements (case studies)
  - Ch 6: Arms Treaties and the Changing Credibility of Preventive War (theory chapter)
  - Ch 7: You Get What You Give: Endogenous Nuclear Reversal (theory)
  - Ch 8: Preventive Strikes (theory + empirics)
  - Ch 9: Lessons Learned
- **Proofs**: Proofs of lemmas (e.g., Proof of Lemma 3.1) appear in the **appendix**, with the main text presenting results verbally and with figures. The appendix also extends results to risk-averse states.
- **Accessibility strategy**: Each model has a corresponding case study that "operationalizes critical explanatory variables." Spaniel notes that "Game theory provides wonderful accounting standards but often lacks straightforward application" — the case studies are the bridge. Intuition sections appear in the main text before formal results.
- **Bridge model→empirics**: Model parameters (rising power's capability trajectory, cost of preventive war) are mapped to observable nuclear proliferation indicators. Quantitative tests use cross-national data on nuclear programs.

### Lessons for exposition
- The alternating theory/empirics chapter structure is very effective — theory never goes more than one chapter without empirical grounding.
- Proofs in appendix; intuition and verbal logic in main text.
- Case studies serve as "translation devices" between model and reality.
- Good exemplar of the "Hirsch & Shotts" style for a book.

## 3. Hollyer, Rosendorff & Vreeland — *Information, Democracy, and Autocracy: Economic Transparency and Political (In)Stability*

- **Citation**: Hollyer, James R., B. Peter Rosendorff, and James Raymond Vreeland. 2018. *Information, Democracy, and Autocracy: Economic Transparency and Political (In)Stability*. Cambridge University Press.
- **Press**: Cambridge UP
- **Topic**: How economic data transparency affects regime stability.

### Structure and model presentation
- **Chapter 5 ("Transparency and (In)stability — the Theory")** presents the formal model, which uses a **global games** framework (Carlsson & Van Damme 1993; Morris & Shin 2003). This is a single dedicated theory chapter.
- **Measurement chapters precede theory**: Chapters 2-4 develop the HRV Index of Transparency and validate it against other measures. The reader understands the empirical object *before* seeing the model.
- **Empirical chapters follow theory**: Chapters 6-7 provide descriptive evidence and regression analyses testing the model's predictions. Chapters 8-10 extend to investment, democratic dissemination, and autocratic disclosure.
- **Proofs**: Based on the global games approach, the formal derivations are likely in appendices or endnotes, with the main text focusing on propositions and intuition (typical for the global games literature which has heavy technical machinery).
- **Bridge model→empirics**: The model's key variable — precision of public signals about regime quality — maps directly to the HRV Index (count of World Bank data series a country reports). This is an unusually clean mapping from theory to measurement.

### Lessons for exposition
- One dedicated theory chapter, preceded by measurement, followed by empirics.
- The measurement innovation (HRV Index) *is itself* a major contribution, which makes the bridge to empirics natural.
- Structure: measurement → theory → evidence → extensions.

## 4. Acharya & Lee — *The Cartel System of States: An Economic Theory of International Politics*

- **Citation**: Acharya, Avidit, and Alexander Lee. 2022. *The Cartel System of States: An Economic Theory of International Politics*. Oxford University Press.
- **Press**: Oxford UP
- **Topic**: Why the territorial state system is stable — modeled as an economic cartel dividing the "market for governance."

### Structure and model presentation
- **Chapter 2 ("The Cartel Model")** presents the formal model in the main text, including a section on "A Simple Model" and discussion of model assumptions.
- **Chapters 3-6** are historical/empirical chapters applying the model to different periods and challenges (emergence, expansion, challenges, resilience).
- **Chapter 7** brings the model to contemporary international politics.
- **Proofs**: The book is 224 pages — relatively compact. Based on available descriptions, the formal model is kept simple enough to reside in the main text without extensive appendix proofs.
- **Accessibility strategy**: The cartel metaphor itself is the accessibility device — readers understand cartels from economics, and the model translates state system dynamics into cartel language.
- **Bridge model→empirics**: Historical evidence (state sizes, territorial changes, warfare patterns) is used to evaluate the model's predictions across centuries of European history. Not regression-based; primarily structured historical comparison.

### Lessons for exposition
- The metaphor (cartel) does heavy lifting for accessibility.
- One model chapter → multiple historical application chapters.
- Compact book; the model is deliberately parsimonious.

## 5. Branislav Slantchev — *Military Threats: The Costs of Coercion and the Price of Peace*

- **Citation**: Slantchev, Branislav L. 2011. *Military Threats: The Costs of Coercion and the Price of Peace*. Cambridge University Press.
- **Press**: Cambridge UP
- **Note**: Published 2011, slightly outside 2015-2025 window, but an exemplar of the formal model + empirics book format.

### Structure and model presentation
- **Part II ("A Theory of Military Threats")** contains the formal game-theoretic model in the main text (Chapters 3-4).
- **Part III ("Elements of Militarized Deterrence")** contains the empirical analysis and case studies (including the Korean War 1950 as a detailed case).
- **Proofs**: The book uses a distinctive **"Formalities" appendix** system:
  - Appendix A: Formalities for Chapter 2
  - Appendix B: Formalities for Chapter 3
  - Appendix C: Formalities for Chapter 4
  - Appendix D: Formalities for Chapter 5
  
  This means the main text presents the model verbally with figures and intuition, while the full algebra and proofs live in matched appendices.
- **Accessibility strategy**: Slantchev describes making "a determined effort to minimize the algebra and maximize the exposition of the logic it represents." The main text explains the model through game trees, figures, and verbal reasoning.
- **Bridge model→empirics**: Model predictions about how military mobilization affects bargaining outcomes are tested with data on international crises and the Korean War case study.

### Lessons for exposition
- **The "Formalities for Chapter X" appendix pattern** is the clearest template for separating intuition (main text) from algebra (appendix). Each chapter has its own matched appendix.
- "Minimize algebra, maximize logic" is an explicit design principle.
- 328 pages with 30 illustrations — significant use of figures.
- The Korean War chapter demonstrates how a detailed case study can test specific model mechanisms.

## 6. Allison Carnegie — *Power Plays: How International Institutions Reshape Coercive Diplomacy*

- **Citation**: Carnegie, Allison. 2015. *Power Plays: How International Institutions Reshape Coercive Diplomacy*. Cambridge University Press.
- **Press**: Cambridge UP
- **Topic**: How international institutions (especially the WTO) reshape coercive economic diplomacy between states.

### Structure and model presentation
- **Chapter 2 ("Theoretical Framework")** presents the formal model in the main text.
- **Chapters 3-7** are empirical chapters testing different implications:
  - Ch 3: Bilateral agreements and state similarity
  - Ch 4: WTO membership as commitment strategy
  - Ch 5: Coercive diplomacy in comparative perspective
  - Ch 6: Agreements and displacement of coercion
  - Ch 7: Reduced effectiveness of coercion (US evidence)
- **Empirical approach**: Combines formal modeling with quantitative data analysis and case studies.
- **Bridge model→empirics**: Each empirical chapter tests a different observable implication of the theoretical framework using different data sources and methods.

### Lessons for exposition
- Classic "one theory chapter → multiple empirical chapters" structure.
- Each empirical chapter addresses a distinct testable implication.
- Mixed methods within empirical chapters (quant + case studies).

## Summary: Patterns in Model Presentation

| Book | Model location | Proofs location | Accessibility strategy | Bridge to empirics |
|------|---------------|----------------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Voeten (2021) | Ch 4 (main text) | None needed (simple model) | "As simple as 2x2 games"; little math | Ideal points from UNGA data |
| Spaniel (2019) | Chs 2,3,6,7 (main text) | Appendix (lemma proofs) | Alternating theory/case chapters; intuition sections | Case studies operationalize variables |
| Hollyer et al. (2018) | Ch 5 (main text) | Likely appendix | Measurement precedes theory | HRV Index maps directly to model's signal precision |
| Acharya & Lee (2022) | Ch 2 (main text) | Main text (simple) | Cartel metaphor | Structured historical comparison |
| Slantchev (2011) | Chs 3-4 (main text) | "Formalities" appendices (A-D) | "Minimize algebra, maximize logic"; figures | Crisis data + Korean War case |
| Carnegie (2015) | Ch 2 (main text) | Not detailed | Standard theory→empirics structure | Each ch tests different implication |

### Key takeaways for model presentation in a book:

1. **Model always in the main text** — no book in this sample relegates the model entirely to an appendix. The model chapter is where the intellectual contribution lives.

2. **Proofs in appendix** — when proofs exist, they go to appendices. Slantchev's "Formalities for Chapter X" pattern is the gold standard.

3. **Verbal intuition before/alongside formalism** — every successful book explains the model's logic in words and figures before (or instead of) algebra.

4. **Measurement before theory** is a powerful pattern (Voeten, Hollyer et al.) — the reader understands *what* is being measured before seeing *why* it matters theoretically.

5. **Alternating theory/empirics chapters** (Spaniel) vs. **block structure** (theory block → empirics block) are both viable. The alternating structure keeps non-technical readers engaged.

6. **A strong metaphor or analogy** (Acharya's "cartel," Voeten's "as simple as PD") can substitute for pages of technical setup.

## Additional books noted but not fitting criteria perfectly

- **Mares & Young (2019)** *Conditionality and Coercion* (OUP) — Riker award winner, but mixed methods without a formal game-theoretic model. Uses field experiments + qualitative interviews.
- **Nathan (2023)** *The Scarce State* (Cambridge) — Riker award co-winner 2024, but primarily empirical (multi-method study of Northern Ghana). No formal model chapter.
- **Queralt (2022)** *Pawned States* (Princeton) — quantitative historical analysis of tax performance 1816-2005, but unclear if it contains a formal game-theoretic model.
- **Dincecco & Onorato (2017)** *From Warfare to Wealth* (Cambridge) — Riker winner 2018. Quantitative database spanning 1000+ years, but model appears to be conceptual/reduced-form rather than game-theoretic.
- **Milner & Tingley (2015)** *Sailing the Water's Edge* (Princeton) — formal model of substitutability + extensive empirics, but published 2015 (at boundary of window).
- **Mattingly (2020)** *The Art of Political Control in China* (Cambridge) — has a "Theory of Political Control" chapter, but theory is more informal/verbal than game-theoretic.
